# Pass: hierarchy (audience fit, hierarchy, main action)

Scope: every page, judged at one phone (390×844) and one desktop (1440×900) screenshot, plus 1366×650 for tool pages. Categories: Audience & industry fit, Hierarchy & CTA. Evidence: screenshots (fold and full page) and `findings/lens.md`.

## Audience & industry fit
- Does the page speak to the audience in the profile: tone, imagery, density, type size, motion level?
- Does each audience's main path have a clear entry and action?
- Industry expectations from the lens met (trust cues for insurance, product art for games, docs for dev tools)? Any lens red flags?

## Hierarchy & main action
- **2-second test** on the first-screen screenshot at 1440 and 390: what is seen first, second, third, and does that match the page's one job?
- **Content priority:** the largest, highest element carries the most important message, not a decorative image or a vague slogan. A logo, wordmark or brand banner bigger than the headline is a common template tell.
- One primary action per view; secondary actions visibly quieter. Two equal-weight actions that lead to the same place are a duplicate, not two choices.
- Emphasis through weight and color before size.
- **What the audience came for comes early:** the product, listings, results or main tool appear within the first one or two screens; flag pages where "about", "how it works" or stats sections push them further down (use the `space-gaps` and `first-screen` findings: empty bands make this worse).
- **Tool and app pages** (editors, search, checkout, dashboards, sign-in) open on the tool. A marketing-style banner above it is a hierarchy finding, not a style choice.
- **Template tells** (unless the brief or PROFILE.md says the look is intentional): identical rounded cards with the same soft shadow everywhere, gradient washes, an all-caps eyebrow over every heading, a generic stock hero, section after section with the same layout.
